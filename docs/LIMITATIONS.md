# Limitations

Read this before quoting any number from this repository.

## 1. All headline results are synthetic

- Every result about composition uncertainty, robust optimisation, active learning,
  calibration and sensitivity comes from data generated by the RobustPrint simulator
  (`src/robustprint/physics/`). The simulator is a set of simplified, documented
  relationships with **assumed coefficients** (DECISIONS.md, ADR-007 to ADR-012). It has
  not been fitted to, or checked against, any measurement of recycled ISS-like feedstock.
- The simulator cannot replace experiments. It sets the *structure* of the problem (blend
  immiscibility, fibre and contaminant effects, thermal bonding, extrusion limits,
  clogging and warping, batch effects, heteroscedastic noise) at plausible magnitudes.
  Real feedstock may behave differently in ways that change every conclusion.
- The benefit of robust optimisation depends on composition × process interactions. We
  added one such interaction (fibre × flow clogging) after a first check showed that
  without it, robust and nominal optima coincided (ADR-012). That is a modelling choice,
  made before any model or optimiser experiment was run, but it means the synthetic
  benefit is partly *built in*. Whether real recycled feedstock has such interactions, and
  how strong they are, is an empirical question.
- Modelled tensile strengths (roughly 2–12 MPa) are low compared with virgin filament.
  This reflects the assumed immiscibility, contaminants and degradation; it is not a
  prediction for any real material.

## 2. Simplifications in the simulator

- One composite "contaminant" class stands for foam, cellulose, food residue and
  adhesives; one "fibre" class stands for all cloth fibres. Real waste has many more
  constituents, and additives and compatibilisers are absent.
- Raster angle enters through a single-angle Tsai–Hill rule, although real parts
  alternate ±θ layers. Infill acts only through the load-bearing section fraction.
- Thermal history uses a lumped exponential cooling model, with no layer-by-layer reheating
  and no part-geometry effects.
- The failure model has two modes (clogging, warping) with logistic forms. Real failures
  include stringing, under-extrusion from wet filament, nozzle wear and filament-making
  defects; the filament extrusion step itself is not modelled.
- Moisture effects (hydrolysis, bubbles) are crude. Molecular-weight loss from repeated
  recycling, which the Refabricator demonstration was designed to study, is only an
  unobserved random batch effect.
- Microgravity effects on printing are not modelled. NASA's first ISS print phase reported
  no substantive effect of microgravity on the FDM process [PRATER-2016], but recycled
  feedstock was not part of that test.

## 3. Modelling and statistical limitations

- Models see 13 inputs; with fewer than ~100 successful jobs, predictions are rough
  (learning curves in `results/full/figures/learning_curves.png`). The GP family
  under-covers on new batches unless wrapped with conformal prediction.
- Conformal guarantees are marginal and assume exchangeable batches. They do not hold
  under feedstock shift (see the OOD rows of `results/full/e2_models/conformal.csv`).
- The robust objectives integrate over a *stated* feedstock distribution. If that
  distribution is wrong, the robustness is wrong too; the mis-specification ablation shows
  how much this matters in the simulator.
- Robust objectives that include epistemic uncertainty are pessimistic where the model is
  uncertain. With little data that can steer recommendations towards well-sampled but
  mediocre settings.
- The unscented transform was cheap but inaccurate for the simulator's nonlinear response.
  Monte Carlo with ≥ 256 draws is the default.
- Statistical tests are paired Wilcoxon tests over simulation seeds with Holm correction
  within each experiment family. Seeds are replications of the *simulated* world, so
  p-values describe simulator variability, not evidence about real materials.
- Only one feedstock scenario (`iss_nominal`) and one shifted scenario were studied; the
  simulator, the hyperparameters and the protocol were all designed by the same author
  (researcher degrees of freedom, partly mitigated by pre-registering the protocol).

## 4. Public data

- The Kaggle dataset has 50 rows of virgin PLA/ABS from one printer, an unknown licence,
  and settings that co-vary by design (bed temperature and fan speed move with nozzle
  temperature). It says nothing about recycled or mixed feedstock. We use it only for a
  trend check and a small benchmark, both clearly separated from the main results.

## 5. Not for flight or safety-critical use

The models, settings and code are **not validated for flight hardware, crewed
environments or any safety-critical application**. Printed parts from recycled
feedstock need qualification testing under the relevant standards (material allowables,
fatigue, outgassing, flammability and toxicity for crewed spacecraft) before any such use.
RobustPrint is a research and planning tool for choosing *which experiments to run* and
*which settings to try first*.
